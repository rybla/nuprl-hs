theory ND

Natural deduction

Depth-indexed propositional formulas and soundness of the ND combinators.

A depth-indexed object logic and the soundness of natural deduction.

Formulas are indexed by a depth `n`, as `Statement(n)` is in the denotational example (Constable et al. §11.6): `Fml(0)` is empty, and `Fml(n+1)` may mention subformulas of depth `n`. Atoms, conjunction, implication and negation are the four constructors. A valuation `v : Atom → U{i}` interprets a formula as a type; each ND rule is a combinator on those types (soundness).

-- | A depth-indexed object logic and the soundness of natural deduction.
--
-- Formulas are indexed by a depth `n`, as `Statement(n)` is in the
-- denotational example (Constable et al. §11.6): `Fml(0)` is empty, and
-- `Fml(n+1)` may mention subformulas of depth `n`. Atoms, conjunction,
-- implication and negation are the four constructors. A valuation
-- `v : Atom → U{i}` interprets a formula as a type; each ND rule is a
-- combinator on those types (soundness).
theory ND

comment book "Depth-indexed object syntax, in the style of Constable et al. §11.6."

abs Fst(p) == let <u, v> = p in u
abs Snd(p) == let <u, v> = p in v

-- Fml(n+1) = Atom ⊎ (T × T) ⊎ (T × T) ⊎ T
--   inl a              atom a
--   inr (inl <A,B>)    A ∧ B
--   inr (inr (inl <A,B>))  A → B
--   inr (inr (inr A))  ¬A
abs Fml(n) ==
  ind(n; x,T. Void; Void; x,T. Atom ⊎ ((T × T) ⊎ ((T × T) ⊎ T)))

soft Fst, Snd, Fml

  decide f of
    inl a => v a
  | inr r => False

  decide f of
    inl a => v a
  | inr r =>

        inl p => Inter1(v; Fst(p)) ∧ Inter1(v; Snd(p))
