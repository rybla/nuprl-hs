theory Existence

Weak vs. strong existence

Σ vs. squash vs. ¬¬∃; the witness is in the extract only for strong existence.

Weak vs. strong existence.

CTT distinguishes several readings of “there exists”:

* Strong `∃x:A. P x` — a pair `<x, p>`; the witness is in the extract. * Set `{x:A | P x}` — the witness is kept, the proof of `P x` is hidden. * Squash `[∃x:A. P x]` — only the fact of existence; extract is `Ax`. * Weak `¬¬∃x:A. P x` — irrefutability; no witness, but no counterexample.

Strong implies the others. Squash implies weak, because `False` is squash-stable (NuPRL §9.12). The converses need excluded middle or a decision procedure (Markov’s principle on ℕ).

-- | Weak vs. strong existence.
--
-- CTT distinguishes several readings of “there exists”:
--
--   * Strong  `∃x:A. P x`  — a pair `<x, p>`; the witness is in the extract.
--   * Set     `{x:A | P x}` — the witness is kept, the proof of `P x` is hidden.
--   * Squash  `[∃x:A. P x]` — only the fact of existence; extract is `Ax`.
--   * Weak    `¬¬∃x:A. P x` — irrefutability; no witness, but no counterexample.
--
-- Strong implies the others. Squash implies weak, because `False` is
-- squash-stable (NuPRL §9.12). The converses need excluded middle or a
-- decision procedure (Markov’s principle on ℕ).
theory Existence

abs StrongEx(A; F) == ∃x:A. F x
abs SetEx(A; F) == {x:A | F x}
abs SquashEx(A; F) == [∃x:A. F x]
abs WeakEx(A; F) == ¬¬(∃x:A. F x)
abs UniqueEx(A; F) ==
  (∃x:A. F x) ∧ (∀x:A. ∀y:A. F x → F y → x = y ∈ A)

soft StrongEx, SetEx, SquashEx, WeakEx, UniqueEx
