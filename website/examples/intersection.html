Intersection

Family intersection ⋂x:A. B and independent A ∩ B. The extract ignores the index.

Family intersection (`isect`) in computational type theory.

A term inhabits `⋂x:A. B` when it inhabits `B[a]` for every `a` in `A`, without depending computationally on the choice of `a`. Introduction hides the index; the extract is the common realizer, not a λ-abstraction. Independent `A ∩ B` is the dummy-binder case `isect(A; _.B)`.

Common elements of two types are a *set*, `{x:A | x ∈ B}`, not `A ∩ B`.

-- A term inhabits `⋂x:A. B` when it inhabits `B[a]` for every `a` in `A`,

-- A realizer of B is a realizer of A ∩ B; the index is not used.
theorem cap_intro :
  ∀A:U{i}. ∀B:U{i}. B → A ∩ B
proof
  intro A
  intro B
  intro b
  intro
  hyp
qed

-- To recover B from A ∩ B one must show A is inhabited.
theorem cap_elim :
  ∀A:U{i}. ∀B:U{i}. A → (A ∩ B) → B
proof
  intro A
  intro B
  intro a
  intro f
  elim 4 with a THENL [hyp, hyp]
qed

  ∀A:U{i}. ∀F:(A → U{i}). (⋂x:A. F x) → ∀a:A. F a
proof
  intro A
  intro F
  intro f
  intro a
  elim 3 with a THENL [hyp, hyp]
qed
